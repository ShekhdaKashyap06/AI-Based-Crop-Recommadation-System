About this project

How Field Ledger picks a crop for your field.

Field Ledger is an AI crop recommendation system. You describe your soil, climate, land and budget. It returns the three most promising crops, prices each one for your land, and names the best crop you can afford.

1

Predict

A machine-learning model reads your soil and climate and lists its top 3 crops.

2

Check the fit

Each crop is scored against its ideal soil, pH, temperature and rainfall.

3

Price it

Cost per acre is scaled to your land and compared with your budget.

4

Pick

The affordable crop with the highest final score is your best pick.

1. The machine-learning model

The model learned from 2,200 field records covering 22 crops (100 records each). Every record has seven inputs: nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, temperature, humidity, soil pH and rainfall.

Five algorithms were trained and compared on the same 80/20 train-test split: Decision Tree, Random Forest, KNN, SVM and Logistic Regression. The one with the best accuracy (F1 score breaks ties) is saved and used by the API. That was Random Forest, at about 99.5% accuracy on data it had never seen.

For your field the model gives each of the 22 crops a probability. The three highest become the candidates, and each probability (as a percentage) is the crop's ML score.

2. Suitability check

The ML score only looks at numbers. The suitability check adds farming knowledge from a table of ideal conditions for each crop, and gives four scores:

FactorWeightHow it is scored
Soil type30%100 if your soil is one the crop accepts, otherwise 0
Soil pH25%100 inside the ideal range, lower the further outside it
Temperature25%same range rule
Rainfall20%same range rule

Outside the range, a score drops in proportion to the distance from the range divided by the range's width, and never goes below 0. The four scores combine into the Suitability score. Then the two scores are blended:

Final score = 65% × ML score + 35% × Suitability score

3. Budget check

Each crop has an estimated growing cost per acre. Your land is converted from square feet to acres (1 acre = 43,560 sq.ft) and the cost is scaled to fit.

Estimated cost = cost per acre × (land area ÷ 43,560) Affordable if Estimated cost ≤ your budget Budget score = min(budget ÷ Estimated cost × 100, 100)

A budget score of 100% means the crop fits inside your budget. A lower score means it uses up more than you have.

4. Best affordable crop

Among the top three, only crops marked Affordable are eligible. The one with the highest final score is shown as your best affordable crop. If none of the three fits your budget, Field Ledger says so instead of forcing a pick. Your budget filters the list; it does not change the ranking.

Check your own crop (step 3)

If you already have a crop in mind, step 3 tests it against your field. It applies the same suitability rules and cost formula in your browser, using a built-in table of the 22 crops. It also asks the API whether the ML model puts your crop in its top 3 for this field.

Under the hood

The backend is a FastAPI service. It uses scikit-learn and joblib for the saved model, pandas for the crop tables and pydantic to validate requests. This page is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript that calls the API. It has two endpoints: GET / reports that the API is running, and POST /recommend takes the field details and budget and returns the three crops with all their scores.

Good to know

  • Crop costs are prototype estimates, not real market prices. Replace them with local, seasonal figures for serious planning.
  • Soil matching uses exact names. A crop listed for “Well-drained loam” will not match a field entered as plain “Loam”, so its soil score is 0.
  • Only the ML model's top 3 crops are considered, out of 22 crops in total.
  • Scores show how well conditions match. They are not a promise of yield or profit. Confirm with a local agriculture officer before you plant.
